## 1. Competitor moves (Feb-April 2026)
[UNVERIFIED POST-CUTOFF for the Feb-April 2026 window specifically.] Defensible up to January 2026 cutoff:
- **MyFitnessPal:** Post-2020 Francisco Partners spinout from Under Armour; pushed hard on AI meal scan and Premium paywall expansion through 2024-2025. Barcode scanner moved behind paywall in 2024 — generated churn backlash. Trajectory: monetizing existing base, not winning Maya.
- **Cronometer:** Stable niche — micronutrient completeness is still its moat.
- **Yuka:** Expanded cosmetics scoring; food methodology essentially unchanged. Freemium + ~$20/yr pro. Maya uses it as a *complement*, not substitute — low defection risk.
- **Noom:** Well-documented retention problems + shaming complaints. Pivoted toward Noom Med (GLP-1 telehealth) in 2023-2024. Key shift — see section 3.
- **ZOE:** ~$60/mo, gut-microbiome + CGM + blood-fat test. Older/wealthier cohort than Maya but *closest* philosophical competitor on "personalized, non-restrictive."
- **MacroFactor:** Renaissance Periodization's app; macro-focused, ~$12/mo, cult following among lifters. Not Maya's aesthetic.
- **Lifesum / YAZIO / Lose It:** Tier-two calorie trackers, no standout 2025 moves.

## 2. PCOS-specialist competitors
- **Allara Health:** Telehealth-first, clinician + dietitian, insurance-billable. ~$60-80/mo cash-pay 2024-2025. This is a *service*, not an app.
- **Pollie:** Hormone-health focused, smaller footprint, more content/community than tracking.
- **Hello Aro, Ovary It:** Smaller/earlier-stage per cutoff — current funding/user numbers unconfirmed.

**Defection risk for Maya:** Moderate but bounded. Allara solves the *clinical* gap (doctor who understands PCOS), not the *daily tracking* gap Fuel Good targets. Honest risk is Maya subscribes to both and deprioritizes Fuel Good if its value isn't visible within 3 weeks. No public retention data for any of these.

## 3. GLP-1 landscape
- GLP-1 prescriptions (semaglutide, tirzepatide) grew dramatically 2023-2025. Analyst notes projected meaningful headwinds to calorie-tracking app DAUs.
- **Noom pivoted to Noom Med** explicitly because of this — tracking-only was a dying category for weight-loss-motivated users.
- MyFitnessPal more exposed than it admits; the "eat less" use case is partially solved pharmacologically.

**Implication for Fuel Good:** *Net positive.* Positioned on **energy + maintenance + anti-restriction**, not weight loss. GLP-1 users *post-drug* need maintenance framework + nutrient-density concerns — Maya-adjacent territory. Risk: if Fuel Good's copy drifts toward weight-loss language. Opportunity: explicitly court the post-GLP-1 maintenance cohort.

## 4. Wearable nutrition integrations
- **WHOOP:** Journal feature tracks behaviors (alcohol, caffeine, late meals) but no first-party food logging. Historical stance: "we measure recovery, you tell us what you ate."
- **Oura:** Added meal logging via photo/AI in 2024-2025 as part of Ring 4 / app expansion. **This is the closest existential threat on this axis.**
- **Apple Watch / Health:** Still relies on third parties for food.

**Maya-specific:** WHOOP first-party nutrition would be a serious threat [UNVERIFIED POST-CUTOFF for any April 2026 announcement]. Oura's meal-logging is the one to actively benchmark against.

## 5. CGM consumer adoption
- **Stelo (Dexcom), Lingo (Abbott):** OTC in US in 2024. Priced ~$49/mo and ~$89/28-day at launch. First non-prescription CGMs for non-diabetics.
- **Levels:** Software layer, ~$199/yr + sensor. Pre-Stelo it was the biohacker option.
- Adoption through late 2025: real but still niche — hundreds of thousands, not millions, of active non-diabetic users.

**PCOS/IR implication:** This audience is *exactly* Maya's cohort. CGM mainstreaming shifts "on track" from calories to *glucose response to food*. Fuel Good should be readable alongside a CGM, not competing with one. Integration is a plausible moat.

## 6. Retention benchmarks
Honest: thin public data. Defensible anchors:
- Flurry / data.ai historical: health & fitness apps average ~25-30% D30 retention; top quartile ~40%.
- The "3-week quit point" in baseline is consistent with published habit-formation literature (Lally et al. 2010) + informal app-industry lore, not a single 2026 source.
- Churn reasons consistently cluster on: shame/judgment, logging tedium, no visible progress, social-feature friction.

## 7. Intuitive eating / anti-restriction segment
- Narrative momentum real: *Intuitive Eating* bestseller lists 2023-2025; Glucose Goddess + Attia have made "don't count calories, understand your body" mainstream.
- No single app owns this narrative — mostly books, podcasts, Instagram dietitians. **That is the opening for Fuel Good.**
- Counter-signal: "anti-diet" creators are crowded + low-monetization. The *app* version at the $14.99 price point Maya tolerates is largely unclaimed.

1. **Biggest real threat:** Oura's meal-logging expansion, not any direct nutrition-app competitor.
2. **Biggest real opportunity:** post-GLP-1 maintenance cohort + CGM-adjacent framing for PCOS/IR.
3. **Maya-specific:** unchanged from baseline — Fuel Good's positioning still defensible, but the 3-week activation window is the whole game.
